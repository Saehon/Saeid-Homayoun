## Structured abstract — research design, not results
We ask whether independently corroborated, machine-readable signals of internal-control and financial-reporting risk provide incremental information about stock-price adjustment and risk transmission beyond conventional textual disclosures and asset-pricing characteristics. Prior research shows that linguistic changes in SEC filings predict stock returns (Cohen, Malloy, and Nguyen, 2020), internal-control deficiencies correlate with financing risk (Ashbaugh-Skaife et al., 2009), and material-weakness disclosures can be followed by return drift (Ghosh et al., 2025/2026). More recent evidence documents LLM-based news return forecasts (JFE, 2026) and machine-forecast disagreement in the cross-section (Bali et al., RFS, 2026). These findings motivate, but substantially narrow, the remaining question: **does independent accounting-evidence validation change which AI-derived risk forecasts are informative to markets?**

We propose a timestamp-safe US filing panel linking public SEC 10-K/10-Q and XBRL evidence to independently adjudicated ICFR outcomes, and—conditional on an appropriate lawfully accessible historical security-return source—to factor-adjusted market outcomes. A specialized probability scorer, finance-text models, and conventional econometric classifiers will assess fixed evidence packets without access to future outcomes. The resulting risk scores will be compared with independent verification of provenance, accounting assertions, contradictory evidence, and review sufficiency. Three hypotheses address verification-adjusted assurance surprises, evidence-conditioned model disagreement and account-exposure risk spillovers. Planned analyses include rolling-origin holdouts, security-level event studies, clustered panel regressions, portfolio tests, transaction costs and adversarial evidence-falsification exercises. The study will distinguish prediction from causal mechanisms and include null and contrary results. **No real market sample has yet been constructed and no substantive statistical findings are available.**

**Keywords:** Financial disclosure; stock returns; internal control; agentic AI; evidence verification; audit risk; market efficiency; machine disagreement; replication.

## 1. Introduction — first pre-results draft
Investors receive a large volume of machine-readable financial disclosures but face a more subtle difficulty than text volume: documents can communicate risk using words that appear credible while the supporting accounting records, reporting assertions, and underlying internal controls vary substantially in quality. Progress in financial language models raises the possibility of screening those disclosures rapidly. However, the economic question is not whether machines can assign scores to disclosures. It is whether *verified* machine interpretations reveal information not already incorporated by investors and not already contained in conventional market and accounting variables.

Three empirical literatures make this distinction essential. First, corporate disclosure and text research finds that changes in financial reports contain information about subsequent returns, operating outcomes and price response (Cohen, Malloy, and Nguyen, 2020, Journal of Finance, https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12885). Second, the internal-control literature associates reported deficiencies with information risk and financing costs (Ashbaugh-Skaife et al., 2009, Journal of Accounting Research, https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1475-679X.2008.00315.x). More recently, Ghosh et al. examine post-disclosure drift after internal-control announcements (https://doi.org/10.1111/jbfa.70012). Third, machine-learning finance methods now yield economic signals from text and forecast dispersion (Gu, Kelly, and Xiu, 2020; Siano, 2025; Bali et al., 2026; the 2026 JFE study of ChatGPT headline predictions). Consequently, generic claims that disclosure text, internal-control deficiencies, or model disagreements forecast returns are insufficiently original.

Our proposed contribution concerns **the interaction between domain-specific evidence quality and algorithmic probability forecasts**. We conceptually separate (i) a model's confidence, (ii) the statistical calibration of its predictions and (iii) independent verification of the underlying accounting evidence. These quantities can diverge: a model may report a precise score while extracting an incorrect amount from an XBRL fact, overlooking a contradictory footnote or substituting the presence of a "material weakness" phrase for an actual auditor or management conclusion. Such cases can lead to apparent informational advantages in a backtest that vanish once timing, provenance and label integrity are enforced.

To examine this mechanism, we propose a source-controlled architecture whose Knowledge Core specifies permissible accounting evidence, audit assertions, provenance and label criteria and whose replaceable Technology Core compares dictionary-based, financial transformer, specialist decision-scoring and general-language-model predictors. An independent challenger tests evidence lineage and contradictions. Outputs are not audit opinions and cannot pass human approval automatically. This creates a systematic way to ask whether apparent model disagreement reflects complementary financial information or merely failure to recover the correct facts.

Three testable predictions follow. First, conditional on the conventional risk and disclosure information set, a separately verified surprise in modeled assurance risk may predict later asset-price adjustment and future control outcomes. Second, apparent disagreement is likely to have different implications when claims are corroborated versus when the model comparisons are based on unreliable evidence. Third, pre-existing accounting-exposure links among firms may transmit verified assurance surprises in ways that cannot be explained by simple same-industry price comovement. We emphasize that these are **empirical proposals, not prior findings**; established research on model disagreement and ICFR spillovers makes their incremental specifications particularly important.

## 2. Hypothesis development
**H1 — Incremental verified risk:** Lower subsequent factor-adjusted returns (or greater eventual disclosure risk) are associated with positive independently verified, previously unpriced assurance-risk surprises, controlling for disclosed material weaknesses, return predictors and textual novelty. Null: beta_ARIS=0. Risk-based competing alternative: risk should command higher expected future returns; negative conditional realized returns could reflect correction of mispricing, so the sign is not mechanically guaranteed.

**H2 — Evidence-conditioned disagreement:** The association of same-evidence, separately calibrated accounting-risk model disagreement with subsequent return volatility and delayed adjustment is larger where independent source verification is strong. Null: beta_(D×E)=0. Competing alternative: disagreement from unverifiable source packets has more apparent predictive power because errors correlate with complex firms. Disentangle signal, selection, complexity and limits-to-arbitrage. The 2026 RFS paper *Machine Forecast Disagreement* rules out novelty of generic disagreement alone.

**H3 — Account-exposure information spillovers:** Unexpected verified assurance-risk signals originating at an issuer are associated with subsequent abnormal information updates among peers connected by pre-existing accounting-account exposure graphs, incrementally to industry news and contemporaneous systematic factors. Null: beta_PeerShock=0. Explicitly benchmark against prior ICFR contagion research; a discovered correlation does not prove a causal information pathway.

## 3. Sample, data and pre-registered variables
- SEC filing event time, CIK, accession, fiscal period, filing type; preserve original document and acceptance timestamp. 10-K Item 9A and 10-Q control disclosures require section-specific manual validation. Track SOX 302 vs 404 requirements and auditor attestation applicability.
- Text controls: ND SRAF LM dictionary, 10-K text change and readability; Analytext sections and notes where usage rights are confirmed; SEFD parser in a separate transformer pipeline.
- Fundamentals: SEC XBRL CompanyFacts with dimension/footnote gaps documented; liquidity, accruals, size/book-to-market/momentum/volatility using available-time variables.
- Return data and security joins: rigorous historical identifier mapping, inclusion of delisted firms and corporate actions. **No high-quality open firm-return panel is confirmed; feasibility remains a blocking issue for the Journal of Finance version.**
- Factor controls: Fama–French daily/monthly risk-free, FF5 and momentum, plus selected JKP factor portfolios if frequency and data rights match. Damodaran only industry/annual aggregates, not firm-level data.
- Outcome labels: manual independent adjudication and blinded disagreement resolution; future MW outcomes are external to the candidate model inputs.
- Model families: logistic/elastic net and GBRT, LM dictionary, FinBERT, optional Microsoft-Decision-1 (not yet called), matched-budget generic LLM and governed hybrid plus reviewer. Document model/version/prompt/temperature and whether probabilities are calibrated.

## 4. Identification / robustness and inference
Register event windows before analysis, including [0,+1], [+2,+21], [+2,+60] trading-day windows. Estimate abnormal returns with factor/industry models trained outside the event and cluster by issuer/date where appropriate. Evaluate leakage audits for all signals and security mappings. Keep pre-event earnings-announcement/event contamination flags. Use matched controls, placebo dates, randomly permuted source documents, forged evidence IDs, holdout years, size/liquidity splits and independently adjudicated negative controls. For a potentially endogenous remediation shock, do not claim a causal DiD without treatment assignment and pretrend assessment. Correct three primary hypotheses for multiple testing (Holm or preregistered false-discovery-rate control) and present 95% confidence intervals and economically relevant effect sizes. If total returns and delistings cannot be observed, do not claim implementable investment alpha or use transaction-cost backtest claims.

## 6. Limitations and ethics
Evidence-source independence must be evaluated by humans; an LLM's citation to a shared source is not independent confirmation. POMELO proprietary materials remain private and no research data must be moved to external vendor APIs without rights/security review. Publisher replication attachments and open GitHub code have different licenses, and any high-confidence claims await actual independent replication. A journal submission must report null, contradictory and out-of-sample failures faithfully.
