# NAAIL OpenLab™ — Big Four / Industry Partnership Pilot

## Purpose
This document defines a research-safe university–industry pilot for preparing accounting and audit students to work with AI-enabled professional workflows before entering practice.

NAAIL does **not** require an industry partner to expose proprietary audit technology, confidential client information, production prompts, internal methodology, or private model weights. A partner contributes only approved educational inputs such as a learning objective, challenge brief, guest session, synthetic scenario, or educational proxy-agent specification.

## Partnership proposition

**Industry Partner → Educational Proxy Agent → Synthetic Digital Twin → Student professional judgment → Evidence-governed evaluation → Human Gate → Aggregate partner insight**

The objective is a win–win–win model:

- **Students:** gain practical experience challenging AI recommendations, evaluating evidence, documenting judgment, and escalating uncertainty.
- **University:** receives an industry-relevant, reproducible, research-enabled teaching environment.
- **Industry partner:** helps shape AI-ready future recruits without exposing confidential systems or client data.
- **NAAIL:** provides the neutral simulation, evidence-governance, evaluation, reproducibility, and Human Gate infrastructure.

## Pilot 001 design
Recommended first institutional pilot:

- one university course;
- one participating industry partner;
- approximately 20–40 master-level students;
- 4–6 week teaching window;
- three synthetic Client XYZ Digital Twin cases;
- one Educational Proxy Agent;
- instructor dashboard;
- aggregate anonymized partner dashboard;
- pre/post learning evaluation;
- explicit Human Gate.

### Initial Digital Twin cases
1. Revenue Recognition & Cut-off
2. Goodwill Impairment
3. ICFR / Control Deficiency

These cases reuse the governed synthetic benchmark family already present in NAAIL OpenLab.

## Student workflow
For each case, the student must:

1. review the synthetic client context and risk;
2. inspect the Educational Proxy Agent recommendation;
3. identify relevant assertions;
4. select and evaluate evidence;
5. identify contradictory evidence;
6. propose or modify audit procedures;
7. document professional rationale;
8. independently verify the AI recommendation;
9. choose one governed decision state;
10. submit the conclusion to the Human Gate.

Allowed decision states:

- `ACCEPT_AGENT`
- `MODIFY_AGENT`
- `REJECT_AGENT`
- `REQUEST_MORE_EVIDENCE`
- `ESCALATE_TO_HUMAN`

## Evaluation framework
### Professional judgment metrics
- **RPA** — Risk–Procedure Alignment
- **AA** — Assertion Alignment
- **EG** — Evidence Grounding
- **PS** — Professional Skepticism
- **DS** — Documentation Sufficiency
- **DIST** — Decision Integrity / Stability

### AI-readiness metrics
- **AIV** — AI Verification
- **CER** — Contradictory Evidence Recognition
- **HOR** — Human Override Reasoning
- **ESC** — Escalation Judgment

These metrics are educational and research constructs. They are **not validated for automated employment decisions**.

## Industry partner dashboard
By default, a participating firm receives only aggregate/anonymized information such as:

- completion and participation rates;
- aggregate performance distributions;
- common evidence-evaluation errors;
- professional-skepticism patterns;
- AI-overreliance indicators;
- contradictory-evidence recognition;
- escalation behavior;
- learning gains from pre/post assessment;
- qualitative student feedback;
- recommended curriculum improvements.

Individual student information must not be disclosed for recruitment without explicit student consent and appropriate institutional/privacy approval.

## Research design opportunity
The same pilot can support controlled research comparing:

- traditional case teaching;
- general AI assistant;
- single educational audit agent;
- sequential specialist agents;
- governed multi-agent system + Human Gate.

Potential outcomes include professional skepticism, evidence quality, error detection, AI overreliance, decision accuracy, documentation quality, escalation behavior, learning retention, and time-on-task.

No causal claim should be made without an appropriate research design, pre-specified outcomes, ethics/institutional approval where applicable, and transparent analysis.

## Partnership safeguards
- Use neutral labels such as **Industry Partner**, **Firm Alpha**, or **Firm Beta** until a written agreement permits naming.
- Do not imply Deloitte, EY, KPMG, PwC, Microsoft, Google, OpenAI, or any other organization sponsors or endorses NAAIL without written authorization.
- Use synthetic data by default.
- Do not request confidential client data for classroom use.
- Do not reproduce proprietary firm methodology without permission.
- Keep NAAIL private orchestration, private benchmarks, credentials, and patent-sensitive logic outside the public repository.
- Do not use student scores as automatic hiring decisions.
- Require human oversight for consequential academic or professional judgments.

## Success criteria for the first pilot
A first pilot should be considered technically and educationally successful only if it demonstrates:

1. reproducible synthetic case execution;
2. stable agent/evidence traceability;
3. measurable student learning or judgment outcomes;
4. successful Human Gate enforcement;
5. privacy-safe aggregate partner reporting;
6. no confidential-data leakage;
7. documented model/agent limitations and failures;
8. instructor usability;
9. student usability;
10. partner value without proprietary-system disclosure.
