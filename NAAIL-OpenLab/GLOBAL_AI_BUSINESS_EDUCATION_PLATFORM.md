## One-minute answer

### What is NAAIL?

NAAIL OpenLab™ is a global, evidence-governed learning, research, simulation and professional-development platform designed to prepare students and future business leaders to work responsibly with AI in accounting, audit, finance, investment, internal audit, forensic accounting, risk, sustainability, governance and scientific research.

It addresses a practical education gap: business schools teach theory and professional standards, while employers increasingly deploy proprietary AI systems that students normally cannot access before entering practice. NAAIL builds a research-safe simulation bridge using public or appropriately licensed evidence, synthetic Digital Twins, open-source tools, governed agents, reproducible code and mandatory human review.

The educational objective is not to teach students to trust AI. It is to teach them to **use, test, challenge, verify, document, govern and improve AI-supported professional judgment**.

### What can learners run?

NAAIL supports or targets governed Digital Twin simulations in:

- financial accounting and IFRS judgment;
- external audit, CAM/KAM and PCAOB/ISA-oriented assurance;
- internal audit, ICFR and risk assessment;
- forensic accounting and fraud investigation;
- finance, valuation, investment, asset pricing and portfolio analysis;
- management accounting, planning and performance;
- ESG, ESRS, ISSB, GRI, carbon, climate and sustainability assurance;
- ERP/business-process simulations;
- scientific discovery, replication and causal/empirical research.

Public/free evidence may include SEC EDGAR/XBRL, PCAOB public materials, Fama–French, Damodaran, FRED, World Bank, GLEIF, EFRAG resources, OWID, Climate TRACE and other rights-cleared sources registered through the NAAIL Free Data Fabric™.

### What scientific/professional evidence supports each simulation?

Every material NAAIL simulation must follow the **NAAIL Simulation Evidence Standard™**:

**Business-school learning objective + FT50/AJG research + authoritative standard/regulation + professional-body competency + data provenance + Digital Twin + AI condition + assessment + Human Gate.**

When directly relevant FT50/AJG 4*/4 research exists, the simulation should normally cite 2–5 high-quality anchors. When no suitable top-journal evidence exists, NAAIL must disclose the evidence gap instead of inventing or stretching a citation.

# 1. Why this matters for business schools

AI is moving from a separate technology topic into accounting, audit, finance, investment, risk, sustainability and management practice. The strongest educational response is therefore not a single optional “AI for Business” course. AI should be integrated into the disciplines where students will actually use it.

The 2026 AACSB Global Standards for Business Education emphasize quality improvement, relevance, innovation, ethical judgment, global perspective and societal impact. NAAIL is designed to support these directions through research-informed curriculum renewal, experiential learning, responsible technology use, workforce readiness and measurable learning outcomes.

NAAIL can also be used as an alignment framework for other international business-school quality ecosystems such as EFMD/EQUIS and AMBA, subject to each institution's own accreditation requirements. NAAIL does **not** claim accreditation from any of these organizations.

- Strategic Management Journal;
- Academy of Management Learning & Education;
- other FT50 or high-fit AJG 4*/4 outlets.

The Chartered Association of Business Schools Academic Journal Guide should be used as a guide to journal quality, not as a substitute for evaluating the actual paper, identification strategy, sample, construct validity or reproducibility.

## Research Evidence Card™ rule

Each simulation should record:

- 2–5 directly relevant FT50/AJG 4*/4 anchors when available;
- research question or mechanism being operationalized;
- population/context and period of the cited evidence;
- whether the cited evidence is descriptive, predictive, causal, experimental, qualitative or conceptual;
- what the simulation is allowed to infer from it;
- any contradiction, replication issue or evidence gap.

# 4. Authoritative standards and international organizations

## IFRS Foundation / IASB

Use official IFRS Foundation / IASB materials as the authoritative source for applicable IFRS Accounting Standards. NAAIL's IFRS Intelligence Agent™ may support standards-grounded simulations involving revenue, financial instruments, leases, impairment, provisions, business combinations, intangibles, tax, pensions, foreign currency, fair value and related disclosures.

NAAIL must distinguish authoritative IFRS requirements from academic research, professional commentary, synthetic evidence and model-generated reasoning. Copyrighted IFRS text is not republished merely because it is accessible for reading.

## ISSB

Use official IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards and related IFRS Foundation materials for investor-focused sustainability disclosure simulations, including IFRS S1 / IFRS S2 where applicable.

## EFRAG / ESRS / EU sustainability reporting

Use EFRAG and official EU sources for ESRS/CSRD-related simulations, including double materiality, environmental/social/governance disclosure, VSME and digital-reporting workflows. Regulatory status and effective dates must be versioned rather than silently overwritten.

## GRI / GSSB

Use GRI/GSSB sources for impact-reporting simulations concerning organizational impacts on the economy, environment and people. GRI, ESRS and ISSB perspectives must be kept distinct rather than treated as interchangeable.

## PCAOB

Use PCAOB public standards, rules, inspection publications and enforcement/public evidence for U.S. public-company audit simulations. NAAIL must not imply access to confidential PCAOB inspection information.

## IAASB

Use IAASB standards and official implementation material for international audit and assurance simulations. Sustainability-assurance modules may incorporate ISSA 5000 when applicable and effective for the relevant jurisdiction/period.

## IFAC / International Education Standards

Use IFAC education resources and International Education Standards as a professional-education reference for competence development, assessment, ethics, lifelong learning and sustainability-related accountancy education.

## IESBA

Ethics and independence simulations should distinguish AI capability from professional permission. Relevant topics include integrity, objectivity, professional competence, confidentiality, independence, conflicts of interest, responsible technology and public-interest obligations.

## IOSCO

IOSCO materials may be used where relevant for capital-market and sustainability-disclosure context, including public-market expectations around global disclosure frameworks.

## SEC and other regulators

SEC EDGAR/XBRL and public enforcement evidence may support U.S. issuer/accounting/investment/forensic simulations. Other jurisdictional regulators may be incorporated through explicit Jurisdiction Packs™.

# 6. The NAAIL Simulation Evidence Standard™

Every material simulation must declare the following before it is labeled **Research-Backed™**.

## A. Educational purpose

- target learner;
- course/module;
- learning outcomes;
- prerequisite knowledge;
- expected professional judgment;
- AACSB/institutional learning alignment where applicable.

## B. Research foundation

- research question;
- FT50/AJG 4*/4 anchors where directly relevant;
- research design classification;
- evidence limitations;
- contradictory or null evidence;
- explicit evidence-gap status where top research is unavailable.

## C. Standards / regulation foundation

- jurisdiction;
- authoritative standard setter/regulator;
- standard/rule identifiers and version/effective period;
- source URL;
- copyright/redistribution status;
- distinction between authoritative source and commentary.

## D. Professional competency mapping

- professional body;
- competency/credential family;
- target skill;
- level of learner;
- claim boundary: alignment only, not certification.

## E. Evidence and data

- source/provider;
- raw versus derived status;
- source period/version;
- rights/license status;
- hash/checksum where feasible;
- transformation chain;
- leakage check;
- synthetic/public/licensed classification.

## F. Digital Twin and AI condition

- Digital Twin ID and repository path;
- human-only / AI assistant / single-agent / sequential-agent / governed multi-agent condition;
- model/provider/version if actually run;
- tool permissions;
- evidence-access boundary;
- execution status.

## G. Assessment

Possible dimensions include:

- evidence grounding;
- professional skepticism;
- standards application;
- AI verification;
- contradictory-evidence recognition;
- documentation quality;
- communication;
- ethics;
- sustainability judgment;
- escalation judgment;
- Human Override Reasoning;
- decision quality;
- reproducibility;
- calibration and confidence.

## H. Human Gate

Every consequential professional/educational conclusion terminates with an accountable human decision. Student scores are educational measures and must not be treated as validated automated hiring decisions.

# 7. People × Planet × Society × Sustainable Profit™

Every major business simulation should end with a four-dimensional impact reflection.

### People

What are the consequences for employees, customers, professionals, communities and other affected people?

### Planet

What are the consequences for climate, carbon, energy, biodiversity, natural resources and environmental resilience?

### Society

What are the consequences for trust, fairness, institutions, markets, public interest and wider stakeholders?

### Sustainable Profit

Does the decision create durable economic value, productivity, resilience and innovation without ignoring material human, environmental or societal costs?

The purpose is neither profit maximization without constraints nor economic value denial. The educational objective is **responsible value creation under evidence, ethical, environmental and societal constraints**.

## Society

Benefits when future leaders learn that AI capability does not remove human accountability.

# 11. Example: Revenue Recognition Audit Digital Twin™

**Educational purpose:** audit risk, assertions, evidence, professional skepticism and AI verification.  
**Research:** directly relevant FT50/AJG accounting/audit papers.  
**Standards:** applicable financial-reporting and auditing standards.  
**Professional mapping:** CPA/ACCA/IAASB/PCAOB-oriented competencies as appropriate.  
**Evidence:** synthetic Client XYZ transactions plus rights-cleared public contextual evidence.  
**Agents:** Evidence Agent → Revenue/Accounting Agent → Audit Risk Agent → Critic → Falsifier.  
**Student decision:** accept / modify / reject / request more evidence / escalate.  
**Assessment:** RPA, AA, EG, PS, DS, AIV, CER, HOR, ESC.  
**Impact reflection:** People × Planet × Society × Sustainable Profit.  
**Final authority:** Human Gate™.

This is a fundamentally different learning design from asking a general-purpose chatbot to “analyze revenue.”
