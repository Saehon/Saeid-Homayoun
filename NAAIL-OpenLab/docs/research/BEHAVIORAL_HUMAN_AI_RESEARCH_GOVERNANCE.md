# NAAIL Behavioral Human–AI Research Governance

> **PATENT RIGHTS RESERVED — PATENT APPLICATION PREPARATION IN PROGRESS**

**Status:** public, non-enabling research-governance specification  
**Architecture:** NAAIL OpenLab™ — V2026.3 Multi-Agent Digital Twin  
**Permanent cores:** exactly two — Knowledge Core™ + Technology Core™

## Purpose

This document defines public methodological and governance requirements for NAAIL behavioral decision-science and human–AI research. It does not disclose private experiment-control algorithms, treatment orchestration, unpublished schemas, or patent-sensitive implementation.

## Architectural placement

The **NAAIL Behavioral Decision Science & Human–AI Experimentation Layer™** is a supporting research layer, not a permanent core.

- **Knowledge Core™:** behavioral theory, construct definitions, literature, professional-domain context, measurement semantics, standards, and research evidence.
- **Technology Core™:** replaceable experiment runtimes, randomization tooling, analytics, AI-treatment interfaces, logging, statistical/causal tools, text analytics, and Digital Twin execution.

## Research domains

The layer may support research in:

- accounting and financial reporting;
- auditing, assurance, ICFR, CAM/KAM, and professional skepticism;
- finance, investment, valuation, and risk;
- economics and strategic interaction;
- management and organizational decision-making;
- consumer/business behavior;
- ESG, sustainability, climate-risk judgment, and assurance;
- professional education and human–AI judgment.

## Construct governance

Behavioral variables are treated as **observable empirical proxies** within a stated research design. They must not be presented as permanent personality classifications, clinical assessments, or unsupported psychological diagnoses.

Construct validity should be justified through theory, prior evidence, measurement design, manipulation checks where relevant, and sensitivity/robustness analysis.

## Human–AI experiment governance

Confirmatory experiments should document, where applicable:

- theoretical mechanism;
- research gap;
- hypotheses or preregistered questions;
- primary and secondary outcomes;
- inclusion/exclusion rules;
- power/sample-size rationale;
- random assignment procedure;
- treatment-integrity checks;
- manipulation and attention checks;
- AI-system/model/version information;
- evidence provenance;
- interaction logging;
- analysis plan;
- robustness/sensitivity analyses;
- replication/falsification plan;
- external-validity strategy;
- Human Approval for release/promotion decisions.

## Causal-identification rule

NAAIL should not label an association as a causal behavioral effect without an identification strategy that supports the claim. Randomized experiments, field experiments, natural/quasi-experiments, or other credible causal designs should be used where the research question requires causal interpretation.

Predictive accuracy, model confidence, agent agreement, or statistical significance alone do not establish causality.

## Evidence and reproducibility

Research evidence should be linked to governed provenance and reproducibility artifacts. Public or rights-cleared data may be routed through the Data & Evidence Mesh™ and Evidence Passport™ at a high level, subject to source terms and patent-first disclosure rules.

Reproducible research packages should preserve, as appropriate:

- data/source metadata;
- variable definitions;
- code/environment versions;
- model/agent versions;
- randomization/seed information where release is appropriate;
- analysis outputs;
- deviations from preregistration;
- null/failed tests relevant to interpretation;
- replication results.

## FT50 / AJG research-design gate

When FT50 or Chartered ABS Academic Journal Guide status is used for study positioning:

1. verify the **current** FT50 list;
2. verify the **current** Chartered ABS Academic Journal Guide;
3. do not rely on a permanently hard-coded ranking;
4. distinguish journal-status verification from evaluation of an individual paper's quality;
5. use current, directly relevant literature to frame theory, identification, methods, contribution, and robustness.

## Preferred validation stack

A strong NAAIL behavioral research program may combine:

**Behavioral theory → controlled experiment → human–AI treatment → Digital Twin replication → real/archival evidence → replication → falsification → external validation → Human Approval**.

This is a research design pattern, not a claim that every study must contain every stage.

## Text analytics

Behavioral text analytics may examine management, auditor, investor, learner, or AI-generated text using appropriate validated dictionaries, statistical measures, embeddings, or language models. Text-derived scores must be treated as measurement outputs requiring construct validation rather than direct psychological diagnoses.

## Public/private boundary

Public materials may describe research purpose, domains, methodological requirements, governance, and high-level study patterns.

Private/patent-sensitive materials include unpublished treatment orchestration, detailed outcome schemas, measurement algorithms, experiment-control mechanics, agent-routing logic, internal interfaces, claim concepts, and other enabling technical detail pending filing review.

## Research objective

The objective is to study **when, why, and for whom AI reduces, preserves, or amplifies behavioral biases and professional-judgment errors**, while maintaining evidence quality, causal discipline, reproducibility, ethical interpretation, and accountable human judgment.
