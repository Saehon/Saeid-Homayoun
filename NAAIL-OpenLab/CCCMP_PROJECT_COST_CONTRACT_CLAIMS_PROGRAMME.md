**Parent architecture:** NAAIL OpenLab™ — V2026.3 Multi-Agent Digital Twin

2. **Technology Core™**

## Purpose

CCCMP™ extends NAAIL into project-cost, contract, procurement, claims, delay, commercial, project-finance, accounting, behavioral-decision-science, research and education use cases for:

- construction;
- infrastructure;
- energy and power;
- oil & gas;
- mining;
- EPC / EPCM;
- PPP;
- major capital projects; and
- megaprojects.

The programme is designed to support evidence-governed research and education around project decisions, professional judgment and human–AI collaboration.

## Specialist intelligence families

CCCMP may expose modular specialist roles at a high level, including:

- Cost Intelligence;

Detailed internal routing, dependency logic, graph structures, state transitions, entitlement logic, scoring, automated controls and unpublished implementations are withheld pending patent filing review.

At a high level, CCCMP may evaluate public, open, licensed or otherwise legally reusable source families relating to:

- open contracting and infrastructure procurement;

- construction prices, inflation, labor costs and macroeconomic conditions;

A source name in NAAIL documentation is a **candidate/reference**, not proof that the source is installed, ingested, redistributed or licensed for every use.

**Free to view does not mean free to copy or redistribute.** Third-party terms remain controlling.

## Accounting, reporting and sustainability knowledge

CCCMP may connect project decisions to governed professional knowledge concerning project revenue, contract assets/liabilities, provisions, borrowing costs, impairment, financing, leases/equipment, valuation, PP&E, infrastructure concessions and sustainability/climate disclosures.

Relevant standards are resolved through the existing NAAIL Knowledge Core and professional agents. NAAIL does not reproduce restricted standards beyond applicable rights.

## Behavioral Decision Science

CCCMP may use the existing **Behavioral Decision Science & Human–AI Experimentation Layer™** to study observable project-decision phenomena such as planning fallacy, optimism, anchoring, escalation of commitment, evidence search, AI reliance, override and trust calibration.

These are research constructs/proxies—not psychological or personality diagnoses.

Any public research design should follow NAAIL's behavioral research governance requirements for construct validity, causal identification, preregistration where applicable, power analysis, reproducibility, replication/falsification, Evidence Passport™ governance and human approval.

## CCCMP Digital Twin™

CCCMP may support high-level Digital Twin simulation of a project lifecycle from conception and feasibility through design, estimating, financing, procurement, contracting, delivery, change, delay, claims/disputes, completion and closeout.

Simulations may introduce economic, operational, contractual, financing, regulatory, environmental or supply-chain shocks and examine their consequences for cost, time, accounting, risk and professional judgment.

Detailed event-to-consequence mappings, automated contract/claims logic and state-transition implementations are private under patent hold.

## Research, education and industry separation

### Research Mode

Supports causal research, archival analysis, behavioral and human–AI experiments, replication/falsification and current FT50/AJG-grounded research design.

### Education Mode

Supports MSc/MBA/PhD and professional-learning simulations in project controls, accounting, contracts, procurement, claims, project finance and professional judgment.

### Industry Mode

Commercial or enterprise use is **not licensed by the public repository**. Any industry deployment, consulting use, enterprise integration or commercial derivative requires a separate written agreement and authorization.

## Research quality rule

When FT50/AJG status is used for study design or positioning, the **current** FT50 list and Chartered ABS Academic Journal Guide must be verified at the time of the project. NAAIL must not rely on stale hard-coded journal rankings.

Top-journal evidence should guide theory and method, but rankings do not substitute for construct validity, identification, robustness, replication or professional judgment.
