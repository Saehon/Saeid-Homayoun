## Placement rule

If a capability should remain valid when models, software, datasets or vendors change, it belongs in or is governed through the **Stable Knowledge Core™**.

If a capability can be replaced by a better implementation, it belongs in the **Replaceable Technology Core™** or a modular supporting layer.

Examples:

- BSC / ABC / TDABC / strategic management accounting → Stable Knowledge Core™
- innovation theory / Kahneman / contract theory / causal inference → Stable Knowledge Core™
- SEC / FRED / patents / startup / accounting transaction data → Data & Evidence Mesh™
- HELM / LiveBench / LiteLLM / MLPerf / OpenCost / observability / dashboards → Replaceable Technology Core™ or governed supporting layers
- Mesa / oTree / HARK / FinRL / DoWhy → Replaceable Technology Core™
- Management Accounting & AI Cost Intelligence → cross-cutting layer
- Open Model Benchmark & Cost Intelligence → cross-cutting layer
- Visualization & Decision Intelligence → cross-cutting layer
- Innovation & Entrepreneurship → cross-cutting layer
- Behavioral Science → cross-cutting layer
- CCCMP™ → specialist programme
- Business School Digital Twin → cross-cutting simulation layer

## Governance inherited everywhere

Every layer/programme must use, where applicable: provenance, license controls, Evidence Passport™, causal/decision DAGs, versioning, reproducibility, replication, falsification, red-team/adversarial review and Human Approval Gate™.

AI price and benchmark observations require timestamped provenance. A lower cost or higher benchmark score is never sufficient by itself to determine a governed NAAIL route.
