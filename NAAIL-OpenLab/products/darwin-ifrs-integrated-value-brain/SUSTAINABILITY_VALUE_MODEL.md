# Sustainability Value Model

## Principle

Sustainability is not a separate reporting score. It is a value-transmission and risk/opportunity layer linked to economic decision making.

## Double Materiality

The system may evaluate:

- **Outside-in:** sustainability matters affecting cash flows, access to finance, cost of capital, operations or strategic options.
- **Inside-out:** company actions affecting people, society, natural resources and the value chain.

## Six-Capital Transformation Graph™

The model tracks pathways among:

1. Financial Capital
2. Manufactured Capital
3. Intellectual Capital
4. Human Capital
5. Social & Relationship Capital
6. Natural Capital

Examples:

```text
Training
→ Human Capital
→ Intellectual Capital
→ Productivity
→ Financial Value
```

```text
Lower-carbon technology
→ Natural Capital preservation
→ Customer / regulatory response
→ Social & Relationship Capital
→ Financial Value
```

## Sustainability Value Added

A conceptual form:

```text
SVA(a)
= Financialized Sustainability Benefits(a)
- Sustainability Costs(a)
- Residual Externality / Transition Risk(a)
```

No amount already recognized in the Economic Value Model may be counted again.

## Integrated Decision Value

```text
IDV(a)
= EVI(a)
+ SVA(a)
+ Strategic Option Value(a)
- Residual Risk(a)
```

The optimization objective is:

```text
maximize IDV(a)
subject to IFRS, controls, liquidity, capacity, risk limits and strategy
```
