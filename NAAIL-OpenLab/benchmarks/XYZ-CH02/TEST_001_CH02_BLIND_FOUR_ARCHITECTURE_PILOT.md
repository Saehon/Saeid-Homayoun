## 1. Verified case basis

The source Chapter 2 package describes an anonymized XYZ Digital Twin calibrated from a real public-company regulatory matter involving recurring period-end sales acceleration, market expectations and disclosure risk. The distributed case separates real historical mechanisms from synthetic transactions/documents and retains the identity/source crosswalk as a private scholarly verification artifact.

The pilot must therefore preserve the chapter's Construct Firewall:

**Verified Facts ≠ Regulatory Findings ≠ Allegations ≠ Simulated Evidence ≠ Anomalies ≠ AI-Generated Constructs ≠ Predictions ≠ Causal Claims ≠ Counterfactual Estimates ≠ Damages/Economic Estimates ≠ Recommendations.**

## 2. Frozen professional problem

The test asks whether a governed NAAIL architecture improves evidence-grounded professional forensic judgment when investigating repeated period-end revenue acceleration, concessions, control exceptions, disclosure choices and journal-entry risk under strict longitudinal information cutoffs.

The facts do **not** establish fraud. A legitimate customer may accept early delivery and an accelerated transaction may satisfy applicable accounting requirements. The system must rank competing explanations and request corroborating evidence rather than reason backward from a known regulatory outcome.

## 3. Case evidence contract

The Chapter 2 benchmark includes a six-period longitudinal replay plus T0 setup. The documented XYZ Digital Twin contains 480 synthetic customer transactions with fields including requested and actual shipment dates, quarter-end distance, order value, discount, payment terms, approval user, channel/customer cluster, returns, credit override, document version, user, timestamp and source hash.

Key journal-entry anchors:

- `JE-1`: ordinary completed shipment with customer acceptance and complete support.
- `JE-2`: accelerated shipment 18 days before original requested date, with 3% allowance and net-75 terms; receipt independently confirmed and no side agreement initially identified.
- `JE-3`: $890,000 manual top-side revenue entry at 23:47 on quarter-end by a privileged user, lacking normal support/second-level approval and reversed next period.

Control anchors:

- `C-01`: immutable requested-date control; documented post-approval edits.
- `C-02`: independent approval for large or >10-day accelerations; documented missing approvals.
- `C-03`: disclosure escalation for recurring trend-affecting practices; no formal Q3–Q5 escalation record in the case design.
- `C-04`: second-person/source-document control for privileged period-end entries; JE-3 fails this control.

## 4. Information-leakage firewall

At each replay period `t`, the tested architecture receives only information available at or before `t`. Future enforcement findings, later restatements, interviews, private regulatory crosswalks, instructor solution, hidden gold ordering and later-period evidence remain sealed.

Every condition must record before advancing:

- anomaly/risk output;
- hypothesis ordering;
- uncertainty;
- evidence used;
- missing evidence;
- requested next procedure;
- escalation recommendation;
- output hash/run manifest.

A leakage event invalidates that run and is recorded as a failed experiment.

## 5. Competing hypotheses

Use a common hypothesis tournament across architectures. The exact labels/descriptions must be frozen before execution and mapped to the protected instructor framework. At minimum the set must preserve benign/operational explanations, repeated revenue-timing/control-risk explanations and higher-intent explanations as distinct hypotheses.

The public pilot does **not** expose the private regulatory crosswalk or protected gold package.

## 6. Four architecture arms

- **A0 — Deterministic:** fixed rules/analytics and fixed decision protocol.
- **A1 — Single Agent:** one model/agent completes the task from the permitted evidence.
- **A2 — Sequential Agents:** fixed specialist sequence with forward handoffs.
- **A3 — Governed NAAIL:** specialist analysis + evidence governance + competing-hypothesis review + critic/red-team + verification + Professional Decision DAG™ + Human Gate boundary.

All arms receive the same permitted evidence at each time cutoff. Architecture is the intervention; evidence access is not.

## 7. Repeated-run design

Calibration target: **5 independent runs per architecture**, giving 20 primary pilot runs, subject to identical evidence/task contracts and frozen runtime configuration rules.

The primary unit is:

`Case × Replay Period × Architecture × Run`

Blinded professional evaluation is added as:

`Case × Replay Period × Architecture × Run × Evaluator`

`Evidence → anomaly/red flag → assertion/professional issue → competing explanation → procedure/test → finding → uncertainty → judgment → recommended action`

The DAG is evaluated independently from prose quality.

## 11. Causal firewall

Correlation between targets, early shipment and reported growth does not establish causation. Unless a credible identification design is available, the required output is:

`CAUSAL CLAIM: BLOCKED`

Mechanical revenue-timing sensitivity must not be represented as true revenue, fraud-free revenue, causal but-for revenue or damages.

## 12. Falsification suite

After the clean baseline, run prespecified challenges such as:

- remove nonessential evidence;
- introduce plausible benign distractors;
- withhold a corroborating item;
- vary evidence order while preserving cutoff;
- challenge source independence;
- test omitted seasonality/operational explanations;
- repeat runs under identical configuration;
- substitute eligible runtime/model while holding architecture constant;
- threshold sensitivity for escalation/detection lead time.

Null and negative A3 effects remain valid scientific outcomes.
