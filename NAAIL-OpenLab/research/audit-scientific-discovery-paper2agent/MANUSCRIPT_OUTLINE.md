# Proposed FT50 research manuscript — PRE-EMPIRICAL OUTLINE
**Title (provisional):** From Replicable Audit Models to Evidence-Governed Research Agents: An Experimental Evaluation of AI-Assisted Fraud-Risk Triage and Risk–CAM Alignment

**Target outlets (not assured):** Journal of Accounting Research / The Accounting Review. Fit depends on contribution and published prior art as of submission.

## Draft abstract — no outcomes claimed
We propose a reproducible research architecture that transforms published auditing methods into callable, evidence-governed scientific agents. Building from Bao et al. (2020) and subsequent fraud-prediction research, the proposed framework compares a documented standalone prediction baseline (A0), a provenance-gated research agent (A1), an adversarial co-scientist hypothesis tournament (A2), and a bounded algorithm-search architecture with verification and human oversight (A3). We design independent, point-in-time U.S. company-year and account-level CAM datasets to evaluate discrimination, calibration, reproducibility, unsupported evidence statements and professional audit judgment. The study precommits to temporal and audit-firm holdouts, preservation of failed candidate models, and independent verification of every claim. The proposed tests ask whether evidence and computational safeguards improve reliable auditor attention without equating predicted risk with auditor failure. The empirical sample, benchmark reproduction, evaluator results and causal identification remain to be completed.

## Questions / tests
H1: Evidence gates reduce unsupported actionable claims while leaving baseline scores unaltered; may impose measurable review-coverage costs.
H2: Multi-agent hypothesis tournament and benchmark-bounded evolution yield genuine out-of-sample reliability improvements *or fail to do so*, measured with confidence intervals; selection unaffected by final test set.
H3: Independently assessed pre-CAM financial-reporting risk may associate with future adverse reporting outcomes conditional on CAM choice and materiality, but causality requires separately justified identification.
H4 (behavioral extension): Independent AI-to-AI challenge plus human review improves appropriate auditor skepticism and confidence calibration relative to RAG-only interactive papers.

## Empirical tables required
1. Source, data rights, year availability and sample construction.
2. Baseline original-result reproduction (reported vs reproduced), correction treatment.
3. Fraud base rates and holdout classification (PR-AUC, ROC-AUC, Brier, calibration, Recall@K).
4. Audit risk versus CAM expected/actual topic mapping, including non-CAM cases with caveats.
5. Agent architecture randomized comparison and unsupported claims.
6. False-positive/negative mechanisms, data-leakage diagnostics and counterfactuals.
7. Independent replication, cross-auditor external tests and economically meaningful effect sizes.

- No-CAM flag not audit standard violation; PCAOB AS 3101 criteria.
- Current synthetic benchmark cannot support significance, novelty, audit effectiveness or paper acceptance.
