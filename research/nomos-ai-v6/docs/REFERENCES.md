# Scholarly references and replication sources

The following sources **inform the NOMOS V6 research design**. Their publication does not validate NOMOS V6, and this package is not a full replication of their results.

1. **Chen, A. Y., & Zimmermann, T. (2022).** Open Source Cross-Sectional Asset Pricing. *Critical Finance Review*, 11(2), 207–264. https://doi.org/10.1561/104.00000112. [Replication code and original research files](https://github.com/OpenSourceAP/CrossSection); [versioned data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/OpenSourceAP/CrossSection). **The source article itself is not in FT50.**
2. **Fama, E. F., & French, K. R. (2015).** A Five-Factor Asset Pricing Model. *Journal of Financial Economics*, 116(1), 1–22. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2014.10.010. [Data library](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html).
3. **Gu, S., Kelly, B., & Xiu, D. (2020).** Empirical Asset Pricing via Machine Learning. *Review of Financial Studies*, 33(5), 2223–2273. https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhaa009.
4. **McLean, R. D., & Pontiff, J. (2016).** Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability? *Journal of Finance*, 71(1), 5–32. https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12365.
5. **Bertsimas, D., & Kallus, N. (2020).** From Predictive to Prescriptive Analytics. *Management Science*, 66(3), 1025–1044. https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2018.3253.
6. **Tidy Finance research datasets (2026).** [Factor library: portfolio returns, turnover, costs](https://huggingface.co/datasets/tidy-finance/factor-library) and [portfolio specification grid](https://huggingface.co/datasets/tidy-finance/factor-library-grid). Consult the datasets' versioned READMEs for working-paper authors, dataset construction and usage conditions.

## How the literature is used

- Chen–Zimmermann: source provenance, revisions and published portfolio data.
- Fama–French: factor benchmark and units; the current downloaded file is not an archival as-of vintage.
- Gu–Kelly–Xiu: machine learning and asset-pricing benchmarks for a **future** financial-policy study.
- McLean–Pontiff: concern about publication and selection effects in historically selected signals.
- Bertsimas–Kallus: distinction between prediction and decisions with economic consequences.
- Tidy Finance: specification-level trading costs and alternative portfolio-construction benchmarks, not a universal cost adjustment for OpenAP.

**Journal target:** A future Management Science submission is a research aspiration, not a claim of journal review, acceptance or submission readiness.
