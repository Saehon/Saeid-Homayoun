# ISSB Sustainability Intelligence | Agentic AI Research

**Principal investigator:** Saeid Homayoun · [Research programme overview](../AGENTIC_AI_ASSURANCE_RESEARCH.md)

## Research objective

Study whether governed, source-grounded AI can help analysts and researchers evaluate **sustainability-related financial disclosures**, their internal consistency and their links to conventional financial reporting—while separating disclosure assessment from **independent sustainability assurance**.

**Scientific questions:** Can a domain-specific agentic workflow detect omitted or inconsistent climate-financial connections? Can it correctly distinguish missing evidence from nonapplicability or permitted reporting relief? Can adversarial review reduce unsupported claims of emissions verification or assurance?

## Scope and applicable authoritative sources

**IFRS S1** provides general requirements for sustainability-related financial disclosures, including governance, strategy, risk management, metrics and targets; see the [official standard overview](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/ifrs-sustainability-standards-navigator/ifrs-s1-general-requirements/).

**IFRS S2** addresses climate-related risks and opportunities. Relevant study domains include greenhouse-gas emissions, physical/transition risks, climate resilience and other climate-related metrics; see the [official S2 overview](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/ifrs-sustainability-standards-navigator/ifrs-s2-climate-related-disclosures/).

Both standards have an effective date for annual periods beginning on or after **1 January 2024**, with adoption/jurisdiction, transition relief and amendments requiring separate resolution. Their issue or effective dates do **not** mean that every company worldwide is already subject to mandatory ISSB reporting.

**Sustainability assurance is a different layer:** the **IAASB** issued [ISSA 5000](https://www.iaasb.org/publications/2026-handbook-international-quality-management-auditing-review-sustainability-and-other-assurance) for sustainability assurance engagements. The **ISSB** is a standard setter for **disclosure**. This project will never claim that disclosure scoring constitutes an assurance opinion.

## Proposed agentic architecture

| Research component | Intended output | Scientific control |
|---|---|---|
| Framework and jurisdiction resolver | S1/S2 edition, amendment, relief, adopted reporting regime | No globally assumed obligations |
| Disclosure extraction agent | Source-linked claims and numerical measures | Cite document and reporting period |
| Climate metrics checker | Units, boundaries and disclosed assumptions | Recalculate, retain uncertainty |
| Financial connectedness mapper | Sustainability risks ↔ financial estimates / capital costs / CapEx | Require actual evidence, not narrative similarity |
| Contradiction and omission critic | Opposing evidence or absent source data | Distinguish nonapplicability vs omission |
| Assurance-boundary reviewer | Does a claim concern disclosure or an actual assured engagement? | No synthetic audit or ISSA 5000 signoff |
| Human governance / publication gate | Reviewer decision with unresolved ambiguities | Professional judgment remains explicit |

## Proposed evaluation dimensions

Measure extraction accuracy; missing-disclosure classification (with genuine non-applicability labels); quantitative unit consistency; source-text entailment; cross-period and cross-framework leakage; false positive/false negative costs; expert-rated financial connectedness; and reviewer disagreement. Validate on stratified industry-year/jurisdiction samples only with suitable rights and predeclared methods.

A useful research question is whether **connectedness between IFRS Accounting Standards and IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards** can be measured without conflating different materiality, recognition and assurance concepts. Any causal or investment-relevance interpretation requires a properly designed empirical study, not just a model score.
